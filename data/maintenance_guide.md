# Maintenance Guide

## Pumps

Stop the pump and isolate it from electrical, hydraulic, and pressure sources before opening any housing. Apply lockout/tagout and verify that pressure gauges read zero. Place drip trays below the seal area and wear eye protection because residual oil may escape. Inspect the mechanical seal face, O-ring, shaft, and seat for scoring. Replace the seal when it leaks continuously, the seal faces are cracked, the shaft is heavily worn, or the pump has been disassembled after a previous seal failure.

To replace a pump seal, first disconnect and remove the pump coupling according to the pump manual. Drain the fluid, release trapped pressure, and clean the shaft without filing it aggressively. Remove the old seal and inspect the sleeve for scratches. Lightly lubricate compatible elastomer parts with the fluid recommended by the manufacturer, never petroleum grease unless approved. Install the seal squarely and confirm that the spring and stationary face are seated. Refit the cover, reconnect the coupling, and turn the shaft by hand. Restore power only after guards are fitted and the work area is clear. Run the pump briefly, stop it, and check for leaks and abnormal vibration.

Pump replacement should also be considered if the impeller is damaged, bearings cause repeated grinding, shaft alignment cannot be corrected, or cavitation continues after suction restrictions are removed. Check suction-line strainers, oil level, fluid temperature, and shaft alignment before concluding that replacement is required. Never increase system pressure to compensate for a weak seal.

## Motors

Before any motor bearing work, switch off and lock out the machine. Verify zero energy and wait for the motor and driven equipment to stop. Disconnect the motor leads after recording the terminal connections. Remove guards only from the isolated machine and support the motor so it cannot roll. Use hearing protection when diagnosis requires a temporary run, and never perform energized bearing checks.

Grinding, persistent heat, vibration, or rough rotation may indicate a worn motor bearing. Confirm the diagnosis by checking belt tension, coupling alignment, shaft movement, and lubrication condition. Excess radial movement, discolored grease, or audible grinding under slow rotation is also a reason for replacement. Clean the shaft and housing, inspect the bearing seats, and use the puller and press specified by the bearing supplier. Apply force only to the bearing ring being fitted. Add only the lubricant and quantity listed in the motor manual.

After fitting the bearing, check end play and rotate the shaft by hand. Reassemble the coupling and guards, restore the recorded electrical connections, and perform insulation and continuity checks. Start the motor under supervision and listen for smooth operation. Stop it immediately if vibration, rubbing, smoke, or overheating occurs. Do not force a bearing onto a damaged shaft or use heat beyond the manufacturer limit.

## Conveyors

Keep conveyor drives switched off and locked out before reaching into the belt path. Fit guarding before testing, isolate compressed air, and ensure loads cannot roll back by chocking or lowering the conveyor. Keep hands outside pinch points and place a warning sign at both local and remote controls. Two people may be required when moving a heavy belt or tensioned roller assembly.

Inspect the belt for cracking, fraying, exposed cords, side tracking, heat damage, and damaged splices. Replace a conveyor belt when cracks extend across more than one reinforcement layer, tracking cannot be restored, the splice repeatedly fails, or the belt has insufficient thickness. Check roller rotation, frame alignment, drive alignment, and belt tension. Remove product and debris before adjustment so measurements reflect normal operating conditions.

To replace the belt, release tension according to the conveyor manual, support the rollers, and remove the old belt without twisting chain-driven components. Route the new belt with the manufacturer's running direction marked correctly. Fit the splice, tension it evenly, and align the rollers. Rotate the belt slowly by hand and confirm that no part contacts guards. Restore energy, run at low speed, observe tracking, and stop immediately for an edge drift, abnormal noise, or overheating. Record the repaired belt and tension-setting date.
